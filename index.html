What Is Air Duct Cleaning in San Antonio?

Air duct cleaning is the removal of dust, pollen, lint and debris from the supply and return runs that carry conditioned air through your house. A crew seals off the system, puts the ductwork under negative pressure with a high-powered vacuum, then works each run with rotating brushes and compressed-air whips so the loosened debris travels one direction only — out.

It is not the same job as changing a filter. A filter catches what is moving through it right now. Duct cleaning goes after what has already settled on the inner walls of the ducts, inside the register boots, and on the blower wheel, where it sits until the next cycle picks it back up.

Most San Antonio homes are a good fit for this because of how they are built. Ductwork here is usually insulated flex duct suspended in an attic, not sheet metal in a conditioned basement. That means it lives in a space that gets punishingly hot in July, gets walked on during roof and insulation work, and shares air with whatever the attic is holding.

When Do You Need Duct Cleaning?

You do not need this on a schedule the way you need an oil change. You need it when something specific is going on:

Dust blows out when the AC starts

A visible puff from the registers on startup means debris is sitting loose in the runs, not trapped in the filter.

A musty smell on the first cycle

San Antonio humidity runs high most of the year. Damp dust on a coil or in a low duct run grows microbial film, and you smell it before you see it.

You just finished a remodel

Drywall dust is fine enough to travel the whole system. So is the caliche dust from new construction along the 1604 corridor.

Rodents or insects got in

Torn flex duct in an attic is an open door. Nesting material and droppings in a duct run are a health issue, not a dust issue.

Cedar season hits someone hard

Mountain cedar peaks December through February here. If one person in the house is flattened every winter, the ducts are worth ruling out.

Nobody has ever cleaned them

Plenty of homes in Southtown, Monte Vista and the older South Side still run ductwork installed in the 1970s that has never been opened.

How Air Duct Cleaning Works in San Antonio

Walkthrough and inspection

We open a register or two, look inside the runs with a camera where access allows, and check the attic for crushed or torn flex duct. You see what we see before anything starts.

Seal the system and set vacuum

Every register gets covered and the ductwork is connected to a high-volume vacuum so the whole system pulls in one direction. Nothing gets pushed into the living space.

Agitate every run

Rotating brushes and compressed-air whips break the buildup off the inner duct wall. Flex duct gets a lighter touch than sheet metal so the inner liner is not torn.

Clean the equipment side

Blower wheel, evaporator coil and return plenum. This is the part cheap operators skip, and it is where most of the smell lives.

Optional sanitizing

If there is visible microbial growth, we apply an EPA-registered antimicrobial rated for HVAC surfaces — after cleaning, never instead of it.

Final walkthrough

We show you the same runs from step one so you can compare. If a duct is too damaged to clean, we tell you plainly that it needs repair instead.

Why San Antonio Residents Choose Us

We work the NADCA way, including the parts that take longer

The industry standard (ACR, published by the National Air Duct Cleaners Association) calls for source removal under negative pressure and cleaning the air handler components, not just the duct runs. A vacuum stuck in a register for ninety seconds is not that. Our process follows the standard end to end, which is why a job takes hours instead of minutes.

We know what San Antonio attics do to ductwork

Flex duct in an attic here sags, gets compressed against trusses, and splits at the collar. We flag that damage during the job with photos instead of cleaning around it and saying nothing — a torn return is pulling in attic air, and no amount of cleaning fixes that.

We will tell you when you do not need it

If the walkthrough shows clean runs and a recent system, we say so. We would rather lose one job than sell a cleaning to a house that does not need one.

How Much Does Air Duct Cleaning Cost in San Antonio?

We quote after a short walkthrough, not over the phone, because the honest answer depends on what is actually in your house. Here is exactly what moves the number:

  • Vent count. The single biggest factor. A three-bedroom ranch with nine supply vents is a very different job from a two-story with twenty-two.
  • Number of HVAC systems. Two systems means two air handlers, two coils and two blower wheels — close to double the work.
  • Access. A walk-in attic with decking is straightforward. A low-clearance attic with blown-in insulation over the runs takes far longer to work safely.
  • Condition. Ordinary household dust cleans out quickly. Construction debris, heavy pet hair, rodent nesting or microbial growth all add time and materials.
  • Add-on work. Dryer vent cleaning, coil cleaning or sanitizing bundled into the same visit costs less than booking them as separate trips.

Be careful with any ad promising a whole house for a number that sounds too low. That price usually covers a vacuum at each register and nothing else, and the real quote appears once the tech is in your attic. We would rather give you one firm number up front and stick to it.

Is Duct Cleaning Right for You?

Call us if any of this sounds like your house

  • The ducts are original to a home built before 1990 and have never been cleaned
  • You bought the house recently and have no idea what the previous owners did
  • Someone in the home has asthma or reacts badly to cedar and oak season
  • You have had rodents, or you have seen torn flex duct in the attic
  • There is a smell you cannot place that gets stronger when the system runs
  • You finished a renovation and dust reappears a day after you wipe it away

You can probably wait if

  • The system is under about five years old and the ducts were sealed properly at install
  • You change a decent pleated filter on schedule and see no dust at the registers
  • You had a full cleaning in the last three years with no change in the house since

Not sure which group you are in? Send us a few photos of your registers and attic and we will give you a straight answer before anyone drives out.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should air ducts be cleaned in San Antonio?

Three to five years is the working baseline for most San Antonio homes, which lines up with NADCA guidance. Pull that window in if you have shedding pets, someone in the house reacts hard to cedar season, your home sat through nearby construction, or the ductwork is original to a 1970s build. A home with none of those can comfortably go the full five years.

Does air duct cleaning actually help with allergies?

It helps when the ducts are the reservoir. San Antonio sits under mountain cedar from December through February and oak in spring, and that pollen tracks indoors and settles in the duct runs, where the blower pushes it back out every cycle. Cleaning removes that reservoir. It will not stop pollen from entering through doors and windows, so treat it as one part of the fix rather than the whole thing.

How long does duct cleaning take on a San Antonio home?

A single-system home usually runs two to four hours from setup to the final walkthrough. Two HVAC systems, long attic runs, or heavy construction dust push it closer to four or five hours. We give you a realistic window before we start rather than a number that slides all afternoon.

Why do San Antonio ducts get dirty so fast?

Three local things stack up. The Edwards Plateau limestone under the city throws off a fine pale dust that gets into everything during dry spells. Humidity here sits high enough most of the year that the dust clumps instead of staying loose. And most San Antonio ductwork is flex duct run through an attic, where heat, foot traffic and the occasional rodent open up tears that pull attic air straight into the system.

Is duct cleaning worth it, or is it a waste of money?

It is worth it when there is a specific trigger: dust visibly blowing from the registers, a musty smell when the AC first kicks on, a recent remodel or new build, a rodent problem, or ducts nobody has touched in a decade. Without one of those, a home with decent filters and no symptoms can usually wait. We will tell you on the walkthrough if you are in the second group.

Do you clean dryer vents too?

Yes, and it is worth booking alongside a duct cleaning. The NFPA ties roughly 14,700 home clothes dryer fires a year to dryers, and more than a third of them trace back to a vent nobody cleaned. Long attic or roof-terminated runs, which are common in San Antonio, trap lint faster than a short side-wall vent.
