What Does Duct Mold Removal Involve?

The order matters more than the products. First we contain and clean: the system is sealed and put under negative pressure, and every affected surface is physically agitated and vacuumed out. Growth is organic matter sitting on a layer of dust, and it has to be removed, not coated.

Once the surfaces are genuinely clean, we apply an antimicrobial that is EPA-registered for use on HVAC components — not a general household disinfectant. We treat the duct interiors, the coil, the drain pan and the plenum.

Then we look for the moisture source, because that is what actually prevents a repeat: a plugged condensate drain, a coil running wet, duct insulation soaked from a roof leak, or uninsulated runs sweating in a hot attic. If we cannot find and name the source, the growth returns, and we would rather tell you that than take your money twice.

When Do You Need Duct Mold Removal?

  • A musty or earthy smell hits the moment the AC starts and fades as it runs
  • You can see dark speckling on register grilles or just inside the boots
  • Allergy-type symptoms that are worse indoors than outdoors
  • You have had a roof leak, a plumbing leak, or an overflowing condensate pan
  • The home sat vacant and closed up through a San Antonio summer
  • Duct insulation feels damp, or you have seen standing water in the drain pan

Why Does Mold Grow in San Antonio Air Ducts?

Growth needs three things: spores, organic material and moisture. Spores are in every building on earth and organic material is just settled dust, so moisture is the only variable — and San Antonio supplies plenty of it. Humidity here sits high for most of the year, which means the cold surfaces inside a running AC system stay damp.

The usual trouble spots are predictable. Evaporator coils are wet by design, so a coil with a slow drain stays wet between cycles. Condensate pans hold water when the line clogs with biofilm. Flex duct running through a 130-degree attic sweats where the insulation has thinned or been crushed. And a system that is oversized cools the house fast, shuts off, and never runs long enough to pull the humidity down — which is why a too-big AC can leave a house cold and clammy at the same time.

What Affects the Cost of Duct Mold Removal in San Antonio?

  • How far it has spread. A coil and plenum are contained. Growth through the whole duct system is a much larger job.
  • What the duct is made of. Sheet metal cleans and treats well. Porous fiberglass duct board that has been colonized often cannot be salvaged.
  • The moisture source. A clogged drain line is a quick fix. Sweating attic runs or a roof leak mean repair work before treatment makes sense.
  • System size. Vent count and number of air handlers, same as any duct job.
  • Bundling. Treatment inside a full duct cleaning is far less than booking it separately.

We give you one firm number after a short walkthrough rather than a phone guess that changes once the tech is in the attic. Be wary of any ad quoting a whole house before anyone has seen it.

Cleaning Alone vs. Cleaning Plus Sanitizing: Which Do You Need?

Cleaning alone is right for the ordinary case: dust, pollen and debris, no smell, nothing visible on the grilles. That is most homes, and adding a chemical treatment to it buys you nothing.

Cleaning plus sanitizing is right when there is confirmed growth or a persistent musty odor, after water damage, or when a household member has a real sensitivity. The treatment only works on surfaces that have already been cleaned, which is why we never sell sanitizing as a standalone service.

Be skeptical of anyone offering to fog or spray your ducts without cleaning them first. It hides the smell for a couple of weeks and leaves the growth in place. If your San Antonio home has a smell you cannot place, tell us what you are noticing and we will tell you which of these two you actually need.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if there is mold in my air ducts?

The clearest sign is a musty smell that arrives the second the AC starts and fades once it has been running. Dark speckling on register grilles or just inside the boots is another. If you can see it at the register, assume more of it is upstream on the coil, and have it looked at before summer.

Can I just spray something in the vents myself?

Spraying a disinfectant onto growth sitting on a bed of dust masks the odor for a week or two and leaves the problem behind. Removal has to be physical first; the antimicrobial is the last step, not the only one. It also has to be a product registered for HVAC surfaces, since household sprays are not rated to be circulated through the air you breathe.

Will the mold come back after you treat it?

Not if the moisture source is found and fixed. In San Antonio homes that is usually a clogged condensate drain, a coil that stays wet, or attic duct runs sweating through damaged insulation. We identify the source as part of the job and tell you what needs repairing, because treating the surface without fixing the water only buys you a season.

Is duct mold dangerous?

It varies by person and by the type of growth, which is why we do not make health claims. What we can say plainly is that circulating growth through the air you breathe is not something to leave alone, and people with asthma or allergies usually notice it first. If anyone in the home has a diagnosed sensitivity, talk to their doctor about the health side and call us about the ductwork.

Do you do whole-home mold remediation?

No. We work inside the HVAC system: ductwork, coil, drain pan, plenum and air handler. Growth in walls, under flooring or in a crawlspace is structural remediation and needs a licensed remediation contractor. If we find evidence that the problem extends beyond the ducts, we will say so rather than clean the ducts and call it solved.
